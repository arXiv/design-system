Forms & validation

A field is a label, a control, optional help text, and a slot for an error. That order never changes. .ds-field lives in both stylesheets, so the same markup works in staff tools and on the public site — only the accent color differs.

A form

Real classes from the public stylesheet. Labels sit above their controls, each control is only as wide as the content it expects, and related fields group by spacing rather than boxes.

Validation

A field passes through three moments. Design all three, not just the middle one — most of a form’s life is spent in the first.

1 · Before

One sentence is enough.

2 · Rejected

Give a reason before saving.

3 · Disabled

Locked once a paper is announced.

Four things every field owes the reader

A real <label for>. required and aria-required="true" on anything mandatory, not just the asterisk. The error tied to its control with aria-describedby, so a screen reader reads the problem when focus lands there. And a message that survives the color being removed — red is the second signal, never the only one.

Writing the message

Same rule as the alerts guidance: say what to do, not what went wrong. “Enter an email address, like name@university.edu” beats “Invalid input” — it names the fix, and it shows the shape of a correct answer. Never blame the reader, never use the word invalid on its own, and never make someone guess which of eight rules they broke.

Instead ofWrite
Invalid email.Enter an email address, like name@university.edu.
This field is required.Give a reason before saving.
Password does not meet requirements.Use at least 12 characters.
Error: bad category.Pick a category from the list.

Layout

  • Labels go above the control. A label-left, field-right layout gives a ragged edge, reads badly on a phone, and makes it easy to ship a control with no label at all.
  • Width follows the content. A field the width of a postcode says what is expected before anyone types. Stretching every control edge-to-edge throws that clue away.
  • Group with spacing and a small heading, not boxes or borders. If spacing cannot hold two groups apart, they probably want to be separate pages.
  • The action bar is right-aligned, primary last. On a long form, repeat it at the top so nobody has to scroll to save.
  • Disabled, not missing. An action that is unavailable renders disabled, so the reader can see it exists and reason about why.

Controls that belong to staff tools

Segmented controls, toggle switches, and filter dropdowns are documented on internal form styles. They are internal-only today because nothing on the public site has needed them yet — not because they are forbidden there.

Usage

<div class="ds-field ds-field--md"> <label class="ds-label field-required" for="email">Email address</label> <input class="ds-input" id="email" type="email" required aria-required="true" aria-describedby="email-hint email-err"> <p class="ds-hint" id="email-hint">Used only to confirm your submission.</p> <p class="field-error" id="email-err" hidden>Enter an email address, like name@university.edu.</p> </div> <!-- On failure: add .is-invalid to the control, unhide the error --> <label class="ds-check"> <input type="checkbox"> <span>Email me when the moderators respond</span> </label>
