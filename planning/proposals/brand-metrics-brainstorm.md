# How does arXiv measure brand success?

**08/17/26. By Shamsi Brinn.**

This document is a brainstorm aid to help us identify useful metrics and goals for the arXiv brand. The definition of a brand is simply what outside people think about us. 

## Why do we care?

What people think about arXiv has profound impacts on our ability to provide a good and useful service, raise funding, hire great staff, and progress in directions that our users actually care about. 

Working on the brand does not stop at visual work, though that is important too. The foundation of arXiv's brand is structural. The two most clear examples are becoming an independent 501(c)3 and our longstanding commitment to putting scientists first, not commercial interests. Our brand is how people see us, and our audience is smart. They will see exactly what we are. 

Two examples we have seen first-hand show what a strong brand is worth:

**Researchers give us their time and their honest opinions, for free.** Over the past 35 years arXiv has built a trust-based connection with researchers. In practice that means when I reach out to scientists to ask their opinion, first, we do not need to pay them, which is rare in user research, and second, what comes back is candid. People *want* to tell us how they think we are doing and where we should go next, and they are not afraid to tell the truth. Most organizations pay for participation and still get politeness. We are given honesty. That is a precious resource that our brand bought us, and not one we could buy back if we lost it.

**Our institutional identity was costing us money.** Before July 2026 arXiv sat under the Cornell umbrella, and potential funders told us they were reluctant to give. The hesitation was not about arXiv. A gift made to a university passes through the university's systems, and donors could not see how much of it reached us. The service, the team and the website were the same before and after the spinout. What changed was who people understood they were giving to.

Reader interviews add a twist. Readers did not know arXiv was connected to Cornell at all. Several were surprised to find the logo when it was pointed out, including a computer scientist who had used arXiv throughout a long career and worked at the university hosting it. The association was real to funders and invisible to readers.

So we do not have one audience holding just one opinion of us. A single measure of brand health would average two unrelated things and tell us nothing. So what should we measure?


## How do we measure brand success today?

An interesting historical fact: besides uptime and "keeping the lights on", arXiv has not defined internal benchmark metrics for success, brand or otherwise. It is not a sign of failure, just a side effect of not needing the numbers that most organizations build around.

These are things we deliberately do **NOT** measure: users acquired, users retained, engagement, and time on site are all standard metrics that arXiv deliberately does not chase. Traffic grown organically and our biggest challenge is keeping up, not generating more use. We are also building on a strong, 35-year old foundation that has built strong awareness and trust among researchers in Math, Physics, and CS through years of service, not hype. 

How should arXiv measure brand success? It is a good early question for our incoming CEO and this is the perfect time to ask it (writing this in August 2026). Most organizations are forced to embrace dark patterns or upselling to compete; we have the freedom to build with only user needs in mind. 

## How might we measure brand success?

Setting the wrong metrics will degrade our commitments over time. The right metrics should represent how well arXiv kept our brand promises to users, not how successfully we manipulated user behavior (not a goal!). 

| Promise | Proxy |
|---|---|
| Speed | Global page load times |
| Efficiency | Minimize clicks to core tasks |
| Accessibility | Conformance rate against WCAG 2.2 AA; Traffic shift from PDF to HTML |
| Stability | Uptime |
| Access | More categories, more moderators |
| Privacy | Third-party requests and cookies set on a paper page — target zero |

Did we do what we said? And do users still care about those promises? None of these reward attention-farming, and every one can be measured on arXiv's own systems without competitor data. 

*A note about traffic:* We should continue to measure traffic levels, but not as a measure of success. That is simply a metric that helps us serve our users better.
