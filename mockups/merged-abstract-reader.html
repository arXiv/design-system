Gauge-independent approach to inflation in quadratic gravity

Adrian Palomares, Ilya L. Shapiro, Maria C. Gonzalez-Garcia, Tomohiro Harada, + 18 more

arXiv:2604.22725 DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2604.22725 Submitted 12 Jun 2026 v3 · latest

Abstract

We explore inflation driven by the Starobinsky-like R + R² term using a manifestly gauge-independent approach to cosmological perturbation theory. Working directly with the metric degrees of freedom in quadratic gravity, we derive the evolution equations for gauge-invariant scalar perturbations without recourse to an auxiliary scalar field or conformal transformation. We compute the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio and demonstrate agreement with Planck constraints.

1. Introduction

Higher-derivative gravity has long been studied both as a route to renormalizable quantum gravity and as a source of viable inflationary dynamics. The Starobinsky model, with its characteristic R² term, remains among the best fits to current data [12], yet the standard treatment relies on a conformal transformation to the Einstein frame that obscures the physical degrees of freedom.

In this work we take a different route. Rather than transforming the theory, we work directly with gauge-invariant combinations of the metric perturbations, following the spirit of the Bardeen formalism [3] but extended to the higher-derivative setting.

2. Gauge-invariant perturbations

We begin from the perturbed FLRW metric and construct the Mukhanov–Sasaki variable from first principles. The background line element is

(2.1)ds² = −dt² + a²(t) dx²

and the gauge-invariant scalar perturbation satisfies the standard equation of motion, confirming consistency with the scalar–tensor formulation.

3. Results and discussion

Evaluating at horizon crossing, we find a scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio in agreement with current bounds. The gauge-independent construction makes the physical content transparent and avoids frame ambiguities entirely.

3.1 Additional gauge-invariant variables

We further analyze variables that arise naturally in the higher-derivative theory and clarify their role during and after inflation.
