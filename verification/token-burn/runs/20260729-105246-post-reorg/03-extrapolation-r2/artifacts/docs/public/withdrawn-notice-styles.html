Withdrawn paper notice

A new pattern for abstract pages where an author has initiated a withdrawal. The page below begins with the design rationale, followed by a realistic abstract-page demo showing the pattern in context. Companion patterns: Alerts · Versions.

Design rationale

Each decision below names the system guidance that motivated it. This is a new situation — no prior pattern covers it — so the derivation is from documented principles, per the routing table: "nearest pattern above + docs/BRAND.md; derive from documented rationale; say so in comments."

  • Warning alert (ds-alert-warning), not error The Alerts page says: "warning is advisory amber — 'worth knowing, nothing broke.'" Withdrawal is not a system failure and is not misconduct — most withdrawals are honest corrections. Using ds-alert-error (danger red) would mislabel both the paper and the authors. Using ds-alert-info (calm blue) would undersell the notice for a reader arriving cold from a citation reference. Amber is the precisely calibrated middle: it surfaces immediately, carries urgency, and withholds blame.
  • Placed in the main column, between the version row and the abstract The Alerts placement rule: "Time-critical messages live in the main reading column where every reader meets them — e.g., the older-version warning sits under the title, while browsable history lives in the sidebar accordion. Don't tuck a warning into a rail the reader may never open." A reader arriving from a citation has no context; they will parse the title and then start the abstract. The withdrawn notice intercepts that path before a word of the abstract is read. The Organizing content page reinforces: never put must-see information only inside a closed accordion.
  • role="alert" (assertive), not role="status" Per the Alerts semantics: role="alert" is for warning/error — announced immediately by assistive technology. A screen-reader user arriving at this page deserves to hear "Withdrawn" before the abstract is read to them. This matches the same choice made for the older-version warning (vd-warn, which also uses role="status" in the pattern page but would use role="alert" if the system's own rule is applied strictly — withdrawal is a more permanent and significant change than a newer-version-exists advisory).
  • Tone: factual, attributed to the authors, non-judgmental BRAND.md Voice: "Honest, plainspoken, no marketing gloss." The copy reads "The authors withdrew this submission," attributing the act precisely and leaving no room for arXiv to appear to editorialize. The reason is labeled "Authors' stated reason:" — it is their own words, not arXiv's summary. "Remains available as part of the scholarly record" names arXiv's actual commitment without dramatizing it. No exclamation marks, no blame, no hedging.
  • Author reason displayed inside the alert, in an attributed sub-block The reason is short (1–2 sentences) and is exactly what a reader citing this paper needs. Hiding it behind a disclosure would add a click with no benefit. The sub-block uses a left-border styled with currentColor (the ambient warning amber — no new hex value) to visually separate arXiv's voice from the author's words. The italic face signals quoted text; the condensed-caps label "Authors' stated reason:" uses the system's established metadata-label grammar (--arxiv-font-condensed, uppercase, --arxiv-grey-adjacent). No tokens are invented — the treatment is derived entirely from documented values.
  • Downloads remain visible and enabled The paper content is still downloadable (scholarly record is preserved). AGENTS.md: "Actions disable, they don't disappear." More importantly, scholars who need to verify a citation against the original text can only do so if the PDF remains accessible. Disabling downloads would harm the researchers arXiv exists to serve. No modification to the download button area is made — the alert alone communicates the paper's status.
  • No additional withdrawal signals on the title or download buttons BRAND.md §1: "The interface gets out of the way. Chrome recedes as the reader goes deeper." Decorating the title heading or the download buttons with a redundant "withdrawn" badge would add noise without adding information. The alert, placed immediately between the version row and the abstract, is sufficient. Repeating the signal in the title line or on each button would produce anxious, defensive UI — precisely the dark-pattern register arXiv avoids.

Spectral Methods for Multi-scale Atmospheric Convection: A Unified Framework

Wei Chen, Asha Patel, Marco Rossi

Submitted 10 Jan 2025 · , Revised 14 Mar 2025 (this version)   v1 v2

Withdrawn

The authors withdrew this submission on 14 March 2025. The abstract page and all prior versions remain available as part of the scholarly record.

Authors' stated reason

"An error in Section 3 invalidates the central theorem. The numerical experiments in Sections 4–5 are unaffected. A corrected version will be submitted as a new preprint."

Abstract

We present a unified spectral framework for representing convective dynamics across scales ranging from individual thermals to synoptic-scale disturbances. The approach combines a generalized eigenfunction expansion for the vertical structure with a nonlinear closure for horizontal momentum transport, yielding a computationally tractable approximation to the Boussinesq equations in the limit of strong stratification. We demonstrate convergence properties analytically and validate the scheme against high-resolution large-eddy simulations for three canonical test cases. The framework reduces to established single-column parameterizations in the appropriate limits.

Placement rule. The withdrawn notice goes in the main content column, between the version/date row and the abstract — not in the sidebar, not in an accordion. Downloads remain enabled and unmodified below the abstract. If the paper also has an older-version warning (e.g., the reader is on v1 but v2 is withdrawn), show the older-version warning first, then the withdrawn notice for v2 when the reader navigates to it. The two alerts never appear simultaneously on the same version.
