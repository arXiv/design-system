**Primary content**
- **Page 1:** the eight subject group cards. The reader came to pick a subject.
- **Page 2:** the rows for each category, showing holds and the two touches charts.

**What stays above it, and why**
- **Both pages:** the header (tool navigation, including "All sections" and "Staff reports"). It answers "where am I" and the brief requires it.
- **Page 1:** the heading and its one-line lede. The guidance allows one short line between a page heading and its main content.
- **Page 2:** the report parts with the subject filter bar, the heading "Computer Science", and the views row. These are the navigation layers, stacked as the guidance orders them: navigation, then views, then filters.
- **Page 2:** the view heading, then one short line with the period and Refresh.
- **Alternatives I ruled out:**
  - Tabs for the views: each view has its own address, so links are right.
  - A table instead of cards on page 1: the guidance says a page that sends the reader on uses cards that link.
  - A sidebar for the category list: it would make the charts narrower.
  - The contents bar (TOC bar) for the 40-category jump list: it is always sticky, and the design policies don't approve sticky chrome on this page.

**What I changed**
1. **Page 1: period and Refresh moved below the cards.** The sentence is too long to count as one short line, and the guidance allows this information below the main content.
2. **Page 1: "Subject groups" heading hidden visually.** It just repeated the lede. Screen readers still get it, so the heading levels stay in order.
3. **Page 1: card details on one line** ("cs · 1 archive · 40 categories"), instead of three label-and-value rows. This matches the docs' example for cards that link and makes each card shorter. Physics keeps a short note, "Opens its archives."
4. **Page 2: one toolbar row instead of two.** The sort control was its own form on its own row. It now sits on the same line as the period and Refresh, at the right-hand end, where the table toolbar pattern puts a filter. One GET form keeps every choice in the address.
5. **Page 2: category jump list folded into a closed accordion**, "Categories shown (40)", next to "About these charts". The 40 links took three or four lines above the first row. They are a way to jump down the page, not content the reader must see, and the categories also appear as the row headings.

The result on page 2: below the view heading there is now one toolbar line and two closed accordion rows, and then the first category.
