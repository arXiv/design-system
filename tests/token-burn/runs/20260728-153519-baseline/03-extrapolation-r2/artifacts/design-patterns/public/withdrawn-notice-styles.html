Withdrawn-paper notice

A new treatment for abstract pages where the authors have initiated a withdrawal. No prior pattern existed for this state — every decision below is derived from existing system guidance, not from taste.

The core question is not whether to show a notice — clearly we must — but how to calibrate its weight and tone against the paper's permanent scholarly value.

Design rationale

Each decision connects to the specific guidance document that motivated it.

1. Warning variant, not error — because withdrawal is not misconduct

The notice uses .ds-alert-warning (amber #fff8e1 / #e8b800 / #7a5c00), not .ds-alert-error.

Why: color-mapping.md defines the error/failure variant as "Danger Red #c62828 — destructive actions, error states" and explicitly distinguishes it from Campus Red, which is "heritage only." DESIGN-POLICIES.md reinforces: "Use danger tokens only for destructive actions and error states. Not for brand accents." Withdrawal does not fit either category. The amber warning register already exists in the system for "advisory, not error" messages: mockups/abstract-redesign.html uses the same tokens (--arxiv-warning-bg/border/fg) for the older-version banner (.abs-version-warning), establishing the precedent that amber = "pay attention, something changed about this paper's status."

Tone consequence: BRAND.md on voice says "Honest — We say what's true, including the unflattering parts… No marketing gloss, no overclaiming." The warning amber communicates seriousness without implying the authors did anything wrong. Most withdrawals are honest corrections; the design should not punish researchers for correcting the record.

2. Position: between date/versions and the author list

The notice occupies the same slot as the existing version-warning banner — immediately after the date/version row, before the authors.

Why: CONTEXT.md notes that "readers arriving from a citation may have no idea the paper was withdrawn." The notice must intercept the reader before they read the abstract with the wrong assumption about the paper's current standing. Placing it here — visible without scrolling on most screens — meets that need while preserving the paper's identity: the title and date/version context appear first, so the reader always knows which paper they are reading before they learn its status.

Why not above the title: BRAND.md principle 1 says "the design never competes with the science. Chrome recedes as the reader goes deeper." A notice above the title would make the system's status message the first thing every reader sees — even readers who already know the paper is withdrawn and simply want to download it.

Why not below the abstract: A reader who has finished the abstract without knowing the paper was withdrawn has already formed incorrect impressions of its current standing. The notice must appear before the abstract.

3. Tone: factual attribution, not editorial judgment

The notice says "The authors have withdrawn this paper" — not "This paper contains errors" and not "This paper was retracted." The withdrawal reason is shown in a clearly attributed block ("Authors' statement:") so it reads as the authors' own words, not an arXiv verdict.

Why: BRAND.md on voice: "Honest — We say what's true… Plainspoken — Short words, direct sentences, no jargon-as-decoration." The attribution honors the scholarly norm that withdrawal reasons are the authors' responsibility. arXiv's role is to preserve and surface the record, not to editorialize about it.

The notice text also explicitly states that the paper remains available for download. This is factually true (CONTEXT.md: "the scholarly record is never deleted") and reduces reader confusion about whether they can still access the content they came for.

4. Download actions remain fully visible and unchanged

The HTML, PDF, and TeX Source buttons are not suppressed, dimmed, or reordered.

Why: CONTEXT.md: "The paper content stays downloadable." BRAND.md principle 8: "count the clicks to every core task (download, cite, find the DOI) and minimize them." A researcher arriving from a citation — even knowing the paper was withdrawn — may still legitimately need to download it to understand the context of citations in their own work, or to read the withdrawn result before a corrected version exists. Hiding the downloads serves no one and creates friction on a core task.

5. Non-color secondary cue on the withdrawn version pill

The current version pill gains a small text marker (withdrawn) after the version number. The pill's aria-label also includes "withdrawn."

Why: DESIGN-POLICIES.md (Accessibility): "Never use color as the sole means of conveying information (WCAG 1.4.1). Always provide a secondary cue (text, icon, position change)." The version row is where readers navigate paper history; a researcher comparing versions must be able to identify which version was withdrawn without relying on color or the notice below. The (withdrawn) label also serves keyboard and screen-reader users who navigate directly to the version pills. Color chosen: Library Grey #6b6459, 5.83:1 on white — passes AA for normal text.

6. Author-quoted reason in a nested attribution block

The withdrawal reason appears in a white-background block with a left border (border-left: 3px solid currentColor), separated from the notice prose. A condensed-caps "Authors' statement:" label introduces it.

Why the nested block: The left-border callout shape is already in use in this codebase's documentation pages (e.g., the .callout pattern in alert-styles.html: border-left: 4px solid --arxiv-open-blue). Using a recognizable shape avoids introducing new visual language. currentColor inherits the warning foreground so the border hue stays within the warning palette without a new token.

Why the condensed-caps label: The 11px uppercase condensed style is the established metadata-label convention throughout the abstract page (used for "Subjects," "Cite as," "arXiv ID," etc. in mockups/abstract-redesign.html). Reusing it for "Authors' statement:" places the reason visually in the same register as other structured metadata — factual, attributed, secondary to the abstract itself.

7. No new design tokens — reuse the existing warning palette

The entire treatment uses .ds-alert.ds-alert-warning from design-system.css without adding new --arxiv-* custom properties. The only new CSS is two page-local classes: .abs-withdrawal (a margin rule) and .abs-withdrawal-reason (the nested attribution block).

Why: BRAND.md principle 7: "arXiv is daily infrastructure maintained by a very small team. Designs must be low-maintenance: no patterns that require manual upkeep." New tokens propagate through multiple files (public CSS, dark-mode overrides, forced-colors blocks). Every reuse of an existing component is one less thing to maintain.

Abstract

We present a unified framework for quantum error correction (QEC) protocols operating over distributed quantum networks. Starting from a resource-theoretic perspective, we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for fault-tolerant operation under correlated noise channels that arise in networked architectures. Our main result (Theorem 2) establishes an asymptotic bound on the decoherence time achievable by any stabilizer code family in this setting, extending the Knill–Laflamme conditions to the distributed regime. We validate the framework against three existing QEC schemes and demonstrate that all satisfy the proposed conditions with bounded overhead.

Usage

No new tokens. Apply .ds-alert.ds-alert-warning from design-system.css with role="alert". Add the page-local .abs-withdrawal-reason block for the author statement; style it in the page’s own stylesheet (it never belongs in the shared design system file — DESIGN-POLICIES.md: “page-specific styles stay in the page’s own stylesheet”).

Open questions

  • No reason provided. When the authors do not supply a reason, the .abs-withdrawal-reason block should be omitted entirely. What, if anything, should appear in its place? Options: nothing (just the leading sentence), or a brief “No reason was provided.” The honest-voice guideline (BRAND.md) suggests saying something rather than leaving readers to wonder.
  • Paper with a replacement pending. Some withdrawals are followed immediately by a replacement (new version) — the withdrawal is a transient state. Should the notice link to the replacement when it exists? This would reduce friction (BRAND.md #8) and avoid leaving the reader with a dead end.
  • Dark mode. The warning tokens have dark-mode overrides in design-system.css (@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)), so the .ds-alert-warning base already adapts. The nested .abs-withdrawal-reason uses background: #fff — this will need a dark-mode override (Repository Brown or a dark surface tint) when the public dark-mode pass ships.
  • Heavily-cited withdrawn papers. The notice must not imply the paper is valueless. The current copy (“remains available as part of the permanent scholarly record”) is intentionally neutral. Whether to add anything else — e.g. a link to citing papers — is a separate policy question outside this pattern’s scope.
