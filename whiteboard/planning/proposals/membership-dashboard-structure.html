Moderation statistics: how to organise the content

A proposal for the rebuild of Christopher's staff reports (dev.arxiv.org/activity/). The goal: the same information without nested tabs. Recommendation first, then the evidence.

Recommendation

Keep the Sections overview as the landing page. Make each subject group one report page with a single filter bar at the top and the content as sections on one scrolling page. Put Moderators and Recruitment on their own pages, reached from a secondary navigation bar. This removes both levels of tabs, leaves one Apply button, and keeps every number findable with in-page search.

Grouped by job

  • Scope: group, archive, category. Applies to everything.
  • Time: date range. Applies to everything except the activity charts, which fix their own window.
  • Settings: threshold days. Applies to the holds totals, the legend and two table columns.
  • Overview numbers: the four totals; the per-group figures on the Sections cards.
  • Backlog detail: the category table.
  • Activity over time: the charts.
  • People: Moderators, Recruitment.
  • Recent items: releases and submissions.
  • Explanations: how each figure is counted.

What makes it hard today

  • Controls sit at three levels (page, tab, section) with three Apply buttons.
  • Two rows of tabs hide most of the content. In-page search, printing and screen-reader browsing see one tab at a time.
  • The threshold appears four times: inputs, two totals, the legend and two column headings.
  • The explanations are long paragraphs above the data, so the numbers start below the fold.
  • The date range is stated twice: the inputs and the "Currently showing" line.

2. How trusted interfaces handle this

General patterns, not detailed claims about any one product.

InterfacePattern worth borrowing
Plausible, Fathom (web analytics)One page. One filter bar at the top sets the period and filters for every panel below. Clicking a row in a table adds it as a filter.
Stripe DashboardAn overview of key figures, each linking to a full report page. A date range and comparison period sit at the top of each report.
Google Search ConsoleTotals at the top of a report, a chart below them, a table below that, all driven by one row of filters.
GitHub InsightsA short list of reports (pulse, contributors, traffic), each its own page with one period selector.
GOV.UK Design SystemTabs only for content a user does not need to compare; otherwise show everything on the page with headings.
Our World in DataA short note on how each figure is counted, kept beside the figure and collapsed until wanted.

The common thread: one place to set scope and time, totals before detail, and separate pages for separate questions.

3. Directions

A. Filter bar and one scrolling report

One filter bar under the title: group, archive, category, date range, and the threshold days in a "Settings" disclosure. One Apply button, or apply on change. Below it, totals, then each report as a section with a heading. A TOC bar jumps between sections.

Recent releases and Recent submissions: short lists, "Show all" for the rest.
  • Answers: set scope and time once; scan down for totals, backlog, activity, recent items.
  • Removes: both tab rows, two of three Apply buttons, the repeated date line.
  • Pros: everything is findable with in-page search and printable; the filter bar becomes a reusable component.
  • Cons: a long page; Moderators and Recruitment do not share these filters well and make it longer still.
  • Accessibility: after Apply, a live region states the new scope and total ("Showing cs, 10–30 Sep: 15,324 submissions"). Filter labels sit above their fields.
  • Effort: medium. New: a filter bar container and the stat card.

5. Recommendation

B with A inside it. The Sections overview stays. A group report has a secondary navigation bar: Submissions and holds, Moderators, Recruitment. "Submissions and holds" is direction A: one filter bar, totals as stat cards, then Backlog by category, Moderator activity and Recent items as sections on one page. Explanations move into accordions beside their figures. Threshold days move into the filter bar under "Settings", so the threshold is stated once and the totals and column headings read it.

Moderators and Recruitment answer different questions about people, not about the queue, so they get their own pages and their own filters.

6. Decisions for Shamsi

  1. Direction: B with A inside it (recommended), A alone, B alone, or C.
  2. A filter bar component for the design system: a labelled row of fields with one Apply button, on the warm tint, wrapping on narrow screens.
  3. Apply button or apply on change? Recommended: one Apply button, because the report takes time to load and several fields change together.
  4. Threshold days: inside the filter bar under a "Settings" disclosure (recommended), or beside the table they affect.
  5. Explanations: collapsed accordions beside each figure ("How this is counted"), instead of paragraphs above the data.
  6. Sections overview cards: keep as the landing page, rebuilt with the design system's card grid and the new stat card. The gauges and bars wait for the data visualisation work.
  7. Breadcrumb: drop it on report pages, since the secondary navigation bar shows the place (the breadcrumb rule).
