Secondary navigation: options

arXiv needs a second level of navigation in two places: the account pages (Account home, Submissions, Works you own, Endorse, and for some people Moderation and Administration) and internal tools such as the Admin Console (about 20 sections in six groups). This page compares four ways to do it and recommends one.

Recommendation

Use one component on both surfaces: a section list, a <nav> of links beside the content (Option A). On narrow screens it moves above the content and collapses into a disclosure.

  • It is the only option that fits both cases. Five account sections and twenty grouped admin sections both fit a vertical list. A horizontal bar runs out of room at about six items, and sooner once labels are translated or zoomed.
  • One component is less to maintain. The internal version changes only the accent colour through .ds-internal, the same way cards, switches and tables already work.
  • It is the easiest to make accessible. It is a plain list of links in a named <nav> landmark, with aria-current="page" on the current one. There is no keyboard model to build, no script, and nothing sticks, so it respects the one-sticky-bar rule.
  • It shows where you are. The current section stays visible beside the page, which the header menu (Option C) and the hub page (Option D) do not do.

What exists today

  • Account pages (user-portal screenshots). A second row inside the black header holds Submissions, Works You Own, Endorse, Moderation and Administration as underlined links, with the user's name beside them. A breadcrumb (arXiv › Account Home › Works You Own) sits under the header. The current section is not marked in the second row; only the page heading and the breadcrumb say where you are.
  • Admin Console mockup. A right-hand sidebar of 20 links in six unlabelled groups separated by rules, with aria-current="page" on the current one. It is an <aside>, not a <nav>, so screen readers do not list it as navigation.
  • In the design system. There is no section navigation component. The closest parts are the header dropdown (.ds-site-header-dropdown), the new Account menu, the contents bar (.ds-toc-bar, which navigates within one page, not between pages), and the rail (a sidebar of disclosures on the paper page). The breadcrumb band was tried and removed from the approved chrome.

Option A: section list beside the content (recommended)

A vertical list of links in a <nav aria-label="Account">, on the left of the content, with optional group labels. The current page is bold with an accent bar. It does not stick. Below about 720px it moves above the content inside a <details> whose summary names the current section ("Account: Works you own").

Works you own

  • Fits: account pages and every internal tool with more than a handful of sections.
  • For: scales from 3 to 30 items; groups are plain labels; long and translated labels wrap instead of overflowing; no script; reflows at 320px by stacking.
  • Against: takes about 13rem of width on wide screens; needs the collapsed form on narrow screens (built from the existing .ds-acc, so little new CSS).

A row of links directly under the header, with the current one underlined in the accent. This is close to what the account pages do today, moved out of the black header into its own band.

Works you own

  • Fits: the account pages only, while they have five or six sections.
  • For: familiar; uses no horizontal space beside the content; reads as part of the page chrome.
  • Against: runs out of room at about six items, and sooner with translated labels or 200% zoom, so it wraps into a second row or needs sideways scrolling; does not work for the Admin Console's twenty sections; looks like tabs, so it must stay a list of links and never take role="tab", which would promise arrow-key behaviour that page links do not have; adds a second band of chrome below a header that the policy says scrolls away.

  • Fits: small sets of sections that people rarely move between.

Accessibility requirements for whichever option is chosen

  • A named landmark. A <nav> with an aria-label ("Account", "Console sections"), separate from the header's "Main navigation", so screen reader users can tell the two apart.
  • The current page is marked in the markup. aria-current="page" on the current link, shown by weight and an accent bar, never by colour alone.
  • Links, not tabs. Each item loads a page, so it is an <a> in a list. Tab roles are for panels that switch without leaving the page.
  • Targets and reflow. Every item clears 24px, and the navigation still works at 320px wide and 200% zoom without sideways scrolling.
  • Not sticky. The policy allows one sticky bar, and on long pages that is the contents bar.

Decisions for you

  1. One component on both surfaces (Option A), or a horizontal bar for the account pages and a section list for internal tools? I recommend one component.
  2. Left or right of the content? I recommend left: navigation comes before the content in reading order, and on arXiv the right side already means metadata (the paper page's sidebar). The Admin Console mockup puts it on the right.
  3. Where do Moderation and Administration go? Today they sit in the account row for staff. They lead to internal tools, so they may belong in the internal header instead of the account navigation.
  4. Should the Account menu also list the account sections (Option C as a shortcut)? It adds reach at no build cost, but makes the menu longer.
  5. Breadcrumb on account pages. The portal uses one; the design system removed the breadcrumb band from the approved chrome. With a section list showing where you are, the breadcrumb can go.
